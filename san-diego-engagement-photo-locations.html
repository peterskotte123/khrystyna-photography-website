San Diego Engagement Photo Locations: 12 Spots by Light and Season

Where the light falls, when the crowds thin, where to park, and which spots need a permit. Twelve places I shoot engagement sessions in San Diego, sorted so you can pick one in five minutes.

Updated October 17, 2026. Permit rules below come from the City of San Diego Parks and Recreation, Torrey Pines State Natural Reserve and the National Park Service, and they change, so confirm them before your date. Written by Khrystyna Skotte, a San Diego photographer who shoots engagement sessions on this coast most weeks of the year.

The short version
  • Every spot below is best in the last ninety minutes before sunset. We start then unless the coast is fogged in.
  • Best months for clear coastal light: September through November. May and June are the marine layer months, so the park and bay spots earn their keep then.
  • A couple and a camera need no permit at City of San Diego beaches and parks. Torrey Pines Reserve, Cabrillo National Monument and private grounds are the exceptions.
  • One hour means one spot. Two spots or a second outfit is the two hour session.
  • Shoes you can walk in. Every coastal spot here involves sand, rock, or a trail.

Sunset Cliffs

The classic for a reason. The sun goes straight into the water, the sandstone turns orange in the last twenty minutes, and the tide pools below Ladera Street give you a second scene without moving the car. On a weekday in October it is as good as this coast gets.

Crowds gather along the top railing at sunset, so we walk a little north or down to the pools and have it to ourselves. Parking is free on the street and fills by late afternoon on weekends. Wear shoes that grip; the steps to the pools are steep and the rock is slick where it is wet. A long dress reads beautifully in the wind here.

Sunset Cliffs Natural Park belongs to the City of San Diego. A couple and a photographer need no permit. A ceremony with setup, a gathering over 75 people, or amplified sound does, through the Parks and Recreation Permit Center at (619) 235-1169.

Windansea Beach

Sculpted rocks, turquoise water, and the palm-roofed surf shack that has stood here since the 1940s. The light comes from the side rather than head on, which flatters faces, and the marine layer softens it further from May through July. Surfers are the only crowd, and they are not looking at you.

Park on Neptune Place or the streets above and walk down the sandstone path. Flat shoes or bare feet; the rock shelves are uneven. This is my pick when a couple wants texture and color instead of a wide beach.

La Jolla Cove and Ellen Browning Scripps Park

The one spot where the sunset faces you across the water, so the light hits both of you full in the face while the sea lions bark below. The lawn at Scripps Park has the palms and the gazebo, the sandstone at the Cove has the drama.

Weekends are packed and parking is the worst in the county. Come early, park in the village, and walk. Weekday evenings in autumn are calm. A session needs no permit; ceremonies at Scripps Park are permitted through the City with a capacity limit and one permit a day.

Torrey Pines State Beach and Black's Beach

Long walks on empty sand below three hundred foot cliffs, with low light raking across the water. Black's, reached by the Gliderport trail or a long walk north from the State Beach, is the emptiest stretch of sand in San Diego. Bring a second outfit for the water and a towel for the car.

Park in the State Beach lot off Carmel Valley Road, which charges a fee, or at the Gliderport for the trail down. The Reserve above the beach is State Parks land and requires a special event permit for photography through the State Parks Special Event Office at sd.specialevents@parks.ca.gov, so I keep sessions on the beach and the Gliderport bluff, where the view is better anyway.

Balboa Park

The dressed-up session that does not need a beach. The arcades along El Prado, the Botanical Building and its lily pond, the rose garden, the desert garden across the bridge, and the Spanish Village courtyards all work in the middle of the day because the architecture makes its own shade. On a June morning when the coast is grey, this is where we go.

Parking is free in the lots along Presidents Way and behind the Organ Pavilion, then a ten minute walk. Weekday mornings are quiet; weekend afternoons are not. Heels are fine here.

The City's permit materials cover ceremonies and events in the park, which need a Park Use Permit and allow a maximum of 20 chairs in the gardens, but its public pages do not settle whether a two-person photo session needs one. I keep it to a couple and a camera, no stands or props, and move along if a garden is reserved. If you want a ceremony here, that goes through the Permit Center.

Coronado Beach

Wide white sand, soft evening light, and the red roofs of the Hotel del Coronado behind the dunes. The north end near the Navy fence is quiet even on weekends. The sand is deep, so bare feet beat sandals, and the breeze is steady, so skip the hairstyle that depends on calm.

Park on Ocean Boulevard. A quiet session on the public beach needs no permit. The hotel's grounds and the dune boardwalk on its side are private property, so we stay on the sand. Coronado ceremonies on the beach go through the City of Coronado, which requires a park permit and does not allow roping off an area.

Liberty Station

The old Naval Training Center: long arcades, Spanish Revival buildings, lawns, and the bay at the end of the walk. It photographs well at midday because the arcades give you shade and repeating lines. Free lots, light crowds, and coffee afterward.

Liberty Station is a private development, not a City park, so a professional session there is at the property's discretion. I have never been asked to stop with a couple and a handheld camera, but it is their call, and larger setups should ask the management office first.

Old Town

Adobe walls, courtyards, bougainvillea and tiled stairs. The light is good from midday on because the walls bounce it around, and the colors suit warm outfits: rust, cream, denim. Weekend afternoons are busy with visitors, weekday late afternoons are easy. Free lots fill on weekends, so come early or park on the side streets.

Mount Soledad

A 360 degree view from La Jolla to Mexico, warm late light, and almost nobody. The wind is the catch, so this is a spot for a couple who like a little chaos in their hair. There is a lot at the top. The memorial itself is managed by the Mount Soledad Memorial Association; a session on the surrounding paths needs nothing.

Kate Sessions Park

A sloping lawn in Pacific Beach with the whole city and Mission Bay laid out below, and the sun setting over Point Loma to the right. Free street parking, light crowds, a short walk from the car, and heels are fine on the grass. My pick for couples who want the skyline without downtown.

Cabrillo National Monument tide pools

At the tip of Point Loma, the tide pools below the lighthouse are the best in the county, and a minus tide at sunset is a once-a-season event. Federal land comes with federal rules: the National Park Service requires a photography permit for commercial sessions, with a $100 application fee, a location fee listed at $50 a day for 1 to 10 people, and at least 21 business days to process, and drones are banned. There is also a vehicle entrance fee at the gate. Current fees and hours are on the Cabrillo permits page. Worth it for the right couple, but only with lead time.

Shelter Island and Harbor Island

The bay side, for blue hour. Once the sun is down the downtown skyline lights up across the water, and the masts in the marina catch the last color. Free lots, light crowds, flat walking, and a good second stop after a Sunset Cliffs hour in the two hour session, since it is ten minutes away. No permit for a session.

Which spot for which season

September through November

The clearest coastal light of the year, and on Santa Ana days the air is so dry the sunset goes copper. Sunset Cliffs, La Jolla, Torrey Pines and Coronado are all at their best. Book these months early; they go first.

December through February

Clear skies and low, warm light all afternoon, but the sun sets before 5pm, so sessions start around 3:30pm. Winter minus tides land in the afternoon, which makes the tide pool spots special. Bring a layer for after sunset.

March and April

Green hills and wildflowers on Mount Soledad and at Kate Sessions, long evenings returning, and the marine layer not yet settled in. A good window for the park spots and the coast both.

May through July

May grey and June gloom. The coast is often socked in until mid-afternoon, and some evenings never clear. Balboa Park, Old Town, Liberty Station and the bay side look the same in any weather, so this is their season. If you want the beach in June, we check the forecast two days out and keep a backup.

August

Warm water and the busiest beaches. Weekday evenings at Windansea or the north end of Coronado keep it calm.

What to wear by location

  • Cliffs and beaches: long dresses and linen move in the wind and photograph best. Flat shoes you can take off. Cream, sage, sand, rust and denim all sit well against the ocean.
  • Balboa Park and Old Town: this is where a tailored look belongs. Heels work, and deeper colors hold up against stone and tile.
  • Mount Soledad and Kate Sessions: grass and wind, so flats and something that can blow around without a fight.
  • Everywhere: no big logos, no matching outfits, and bring the second outfit in the car rather than wearing a compromise for both.

Common questions

What is the best time of year for engagement photos in San Diego?

September through November for the clearest coastal sunsets. Late spring and early summer bring the marine layer, so a May or June coastal session may be soft and grey, and Balboa Park, Old Town and the bay side still look good then. Winter is clear with the sun setting before 5pm, so we start around 3:30pm.

Do we need a permit?

For a couple and a photographer with no setup, City of San Diego beaches and parks do not require one. Torrey Pines State Natural Reserve requires a State Parks permit for photography, so we shoot on the State Beach below it. Cabrillo National Monument requires a National Park Service permit with lead time. Liberty Station and hotel grounds are private property. Rules change, so confirm with the agency before your date.

Can we do two locations in one session?

In one hour, one spot. The light changes fast in the last hour and driving eats it. The two hour session is built for two spots or a second outfit, with a sunset finish.

What if it is overcast?

Overcast coastal light is soft and even and photographs beautifully, especially in black and white. If the forecast is heavy fog or rain, the session moves to another date at no charge.

Can we bring our dog?

Yes, and I hope you do. Bring a leash and a friend to hold it between photos, and tell me when you book so I pick a spot that allows dogs. Many San Diego beaches restrict dogs during the day, so the spot matters.

How many photos do we get?

100+ edited photos from the one hour session and 175+ from two hours, in a private online gallery in 2 to 3 weeks, with a few sneak peeks sooner. They download in full resolution for save-the-dates, the wedding website and prints.
