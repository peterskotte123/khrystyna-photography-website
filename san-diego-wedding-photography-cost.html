What drives the price

Every photographer prices the same handful of things, just in different proportions. When two quotes look far apart, one of these is usually the reason.

  • Hours. The biggest lever. Four hours covers a ceremony, portraits and the start of a reception. Six runs from before the ceremony through cake and open dancing. Eight covers getting ready through the send-off.
  • A second photographer. Needed once the guest list is big enough that one person cannot be at the ceremony and the cocktail hour at the same time. I include one automatically over 60 guests. Under 60, it is an add-on at $150 per hour, capped at $1,000.
  • Guest count. More people means more coverage and more edited photos. Some photographers quote by guest count, some fold it into hours. I use one flat $1,000 addition over 60 guests, whether you host 61 or 200, and it applies once per booking even if you add film.
  • Travel. Ask where the free zone ends. Mine covers all of San Diego County and Temecula. Beyond that, travel is quoted in writing within 24 hours as one line item that does not change after booking.
  • Film. A cinematic film is a second craft and usually a second person. Bundled with photography it is cheaper than booking two vendors, which is why my Signature collection exists.
  • Albums and prints. Physical keepsakes are the one place California sales tax applies. An heirloom album starts at $250.
  • Delivery speed. Standard delivery is 2 to 3 weeks. Rush delivery is $400 for weddings and $200 for sessions.

Published averages, and why they vary

I could not find a San Diego average from any primary source, so I will not invent one. What exists is national, and it is second hand. Paperlust's 2026 guide attributes a national average "landing around $3,000" to The Knot's Real Weddings Study, drawn from nearly 10,500 couples who married in 2025, with most couples budgeting $2,500 to $4,500. The same guide reports Zola's marketplace data higher, at an average of $4,400 and a typical range of $3,500 to $5,300. Candid Studios puts 2026 full-day photography at $2,500 to $5,500 for most U.S. couples.

The spread is not a contradiction. Zola's number comes from vendors on a paid marketplace, which skews toward full-time studios. The Knot's comes from couples reporting what they paid, including newer photographers. Use the national range to sanity check a quote, then compare hours and inclusions line by line.

Questions to ask any photographer about price

  1. What holds the date, and when is the rest due? Mine is a signed contract plus a 50% retainer, with the balance due 14 days before.
  2. When does the clock start? On arrival at the venue, or when the photographer leaves home? Travel time should not eat your hours.
  3. What happens if we run long? Ask for the overtime rate in writing before the day.
  4. Where does the travel fee begin? Get the free zone and the rate beyond it on the quote.
  5. How many edited photos, and are they all retouched? A number with a plus sign is a floor, not a ceiling.
  6. When is the gallery delivered, and what does rush cost? Weeks, not months, should be the answer.
  7. Can we print our photos? Your gallery should come with personal print rights.

Common questions

How much is a wedding photographer in San Diego?

There is no published San Diego average. My own wedding collections start at $2,200 for four hours, $3,000 for six and $3,800 for eight, each covering up to 60 guests.

Is $3,000 enough for a San Diego wedding photographer?

Yes. $3,000 is my Gold collection: six hours of coverage, 500 or more edited photos and a private gallery, for weddings up to 60 guests. Over 60 guests adds a flat $1,000 and includes a second photographer.

What does the retainer cover?

A signed contract and a 50% retainer reserve your date. The balance is due 14 days before the wedding or session. You can reschedule once with at least 30 days' notice and the retainer carries over, and unsafe weather reschedules an outdoor session at no charge.

Do you charge a travel fee in San Diego County?

No. There is no travel fee within San Diego County or Temecula. Outside that area, travel is quoted in writing within 24 hours as one flat line item that does not change after booking.

How many photos do we get?

Sessions, elopements and courthouse ceremonies include 100 or more color-edited photos. Wedding collections include 400 or more at four hours, 500 or more at six and 750 or more at eight. Every gallery is delivered within 2 to 3 weeks.

When is the balance due?

14 days before your wedding or session. Rush delivery, extra hours and add-ons are listed on your written quote before you sign, so the total never moves after booking.

Is there sales tax on wedding photography in California?

Not on a digital delivery. Every collection is delivered through a private online gallery, so there is no sales tax on the package. California sales tax applies only to physical keepsakes such as albums, and any tax is stated in the written quote.
