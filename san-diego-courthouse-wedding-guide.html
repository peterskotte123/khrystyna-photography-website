How to Get Married at the San Diego County Courthouse

The appointment, the fees, the four offices, what the Clerk allows, and where to take photos after. A 2026 guide to a civil ceremony at the County Clerk, which is what San Diego has instead of a city hall wedding.

Updated October 16, 2026. Fees and rules below come from the San Diego County Clerk's marriage page and change from time to time, so confirm them when you book. Written by Khrystyna Skotte, a San Diego photographer who covers courthouse ceremonies downtown and at the branch offices.

The short version
  • Everything is by appointment. Book online at sdarcc.gov up to 7 weeks ahead. A new day opens every day, and a greyed-out date is full.
  • You can get the license and be married in the same visit by choosing a "License and Ceremony" appointment.
  • County fees: $129 for a public license (or $144 confidential) plus $107 for the ceremony. Cards add $2.50.
  • Both of you appear in person with valid government photo ID. No residency requirement, and California requires no blood test and has no waiting period.
  • Public license needs one witness at the ceremony. Confidential license needs none.
  • Indoor rooms hold 15 to 20 guests. Outdoor ceremonies have no guest limit. No decor, petals, confetti, or champagne on site.

How to book a civil ceremony, step by step

A civil ceremony, also called a civil wedding or civil marriage, is what most people mean by a courthouse wedding. In San Diego it is performed by the County Clerk, not by a judge or a justice of the peace.

  1. Book the appointment first Go to the Clerk's marriage page and use the online scheduler. Pick "License and Ceremony" if you want to marry the same day, or a license-only appointment if someone else will officiate later. Appointments open 7 weeks out, one new day at a time, and the Clerk asks that you book only one. If a date is greyed out it is full. The phone line is (619) 237-0502.
  2. Fill out the online application After booking, complete the Marriage License Online Application so the appointment moves quickly. A printable version exists if you would rather bring it on paper. This is also where you choose any new last name, under California's Name Equality Act. Decide this before the appointment, since the license is where it gets recorded.
  3. Decide public or confidential A public license is the common one and needs at least one witness at the ceremony. A confidential license needs no witness, but you must already be living together as a couple and will sign a statement saying so. Only the two of you can later order copies of a confidential certificate without a court order. Either license is valid for 90 days anywhere in California, with no extensions.
  4. Bring ID, a witness, and payment Both of you must be 18 or older, unmarried, and present with current government photo ID. If a previous marriage ended within the last 90 days, bring the final judgment. For a public license bring your own witness, or the Clerk provides one for $58. Pay by cash, check, money order, or card.
  5. The ceremony Ceremonies are held indoors, and the downtown office also has the outdoor CAC West Arbor. Confirm ceremony availability at your office when you book. Ceremonies are performed in English or Spanish. If you want another language or a friend officiating, the Clerk's Deputy Marriage Commissioner for a Day program lets someone you choose perform a one-day ceremony. In my experience the ceremony itself takes only a few minutes.
  6. Afterward The signed license goes back to the Clerk for registration, which can take up to 10 business days. A marriage certificate is not mailed automatically. Order a certified copy separately once the license is registered. You will need it for name changes, insurance, and immigration paperwork.

The four offices

Marriage services are offered at the downtown office and three branch offices. The downtown office is open Monday through Friday, 8am to 5pm. Confirm hours at the branch offices when you book. The Kearny Mesa office does not offer Recorder or Clerk services. There is no County Clerk marriage office in El Cajon, Oceanside, or Vista: East County couples marry in Santee, North County couples in San Marcos, and South Bay couples in Chula Vista.

Downtown, County Administration Center

1600 Pacific Highway, San Diego, CA 92101. Recorder/Clerk is Suite 260. Free underground parking for up to 3 hours during business hours, entrance on Ash Street, or metered parking around Waterfront Park. Home of the outdoor CAC West Arbor, with the bay a short walk from the door.

Getting married today or this week

The County Clerk is appointment-only and books about seven weeks out, so "today" is not something the Clerk can do. If you need the license and the ceremony this week, a County Clerk-commissioned notary or officiant can issue a confidential license and perform the ceremony the same day, at your place or theirs. Text me and I can point you to one. The one hour of coverage at $650 fits that ceremony the same way it fits the Clerk's.

No residency requirement

Anyone can marry here. You do not need to live in San Diego, in California, or in the United States. A current passport is accepted as photo ID, both of you attend one appointment together, and the license is valid for 90 days anywhere in California. A license from any California county clerk works for the ceremony, so a couple who got their license in Los Angeles or Orange County can still marry at the San Diego office. The Clerk performs the ceremony in English or Spanish. Verified on the Clerk's page on October 16, 2026.

Temecula and Riverside County

Marrying in Temecula? The Riverside County clerk's Temecula office also issues licenses and performs civil ceremonies, by appointment, and the wine country is twenty minutes from its door. There is no travel fee to Temecula for photography.

What is allowed on the day

Fine
  • A bouquet you carry and a boutonniere, as long as nothing needs placing or setup
  • Your own witness and guests, up to 15 to 20 indoors depending on the office, unlimited outdoors
  • A photographer or a friend taking photos
  • Any outfit, from jeans to a full gown
  • Vows in English or Spanish
Not allowed at the Clerk's ceremony areas
  • Floral arrangements, arches, backdrops, or furniture
  • Tossing anything, including petals and confetti
  • Open containers of alcohol, including a champagne toast
  • Anything that needs setup or cleanup
  • Ceremonies in languages other than English or Spanish

Outdoor ceremonies downtown can be moved indoors for weather, events, or maintenance. The Clerk posts a list of dates that affect the arbor, so check it when you book.

What to wear to a courthouse wedding

Whatever you would wear to any wedding: a gown, a short dress, a suit, or a sweater and good trousers all look right here. From the photo side, three things help. Shoes you can walk in, since the waterfront portraits are a short walk from the door. A layer for the breeze off the bay. And a small bouquet if you want one, because it gives your hands something to do in the portraits.

Where the photos happen

The downtown office is the easy one. The building has marble lobbies and brass elevator doors inside, and the waterfront park, the harbor, and the tall ships at the Maritime Museum are a short walk from the door. Little Italy is close by for a lunch after. Plan on thirty to forty minutes outside after the ceremony for a full set of portraits.

At the branch offices the building itself is plainer, so plan a second stop. San Marcos is a short drive from Lake San Marcos and the Twin Oaks Valley. Santee sits beside Mission Trails. Chula Vista is a short drive from the bayfront. A photographer who knows the area will have a spot in mind. If you would rather skip the building entirely, a beach or cliff elopement with your own officiant uses the same license.

Because the Clerk allows no petals or champagne on site, the toast and the petal toss happen at the portrait spot instead, which photographs better anyway.

Military couples

I shoot around your schedule, with same-week bookings when I can fit them. San Marcos serves Camp Pendleton, downtown serves the Navy, and dress blues photograph beautifully at Waterfront Park with the bay behind you. If a deployment date is driving the timing, say so when you text and I will work around it.

Certified copy of your marriage certificate

The license you sign on the day is not the certificate. After the ceremony the Clerk registers the license, which can take up to 10 business days, and only then can you order a certified copy of the marriage certificate from the County Recorder, in person, by mail, or online through the Clerk's marriage page. That certified copy is what the Social Security office, the DMV, insurers, and immigration ask for. If you need it recognized abroad, the California Secretary of State can apostille it. Order two copies; the first one always goes somewhere.

Common questions

Can we walk in without an appointment?

No. Licenses and ceremonies are by appointment only. Book online, up to 7 weeks ahead.

Can we get married the same day we get the license?

Yes. Choose the "License and Ceremony" appointment type and the Clerk issues the license and performs the ceremony in the same visit. California has no waiting period.

How far ahead do we need to book?

Appointments open up to 7 weeks out and a new day opens daily. If you want a Friday or a date near a holiday, book the day it opens.

Do we need to live in San Diego or California?

No. There is no residency requirement. Couples visiting from out of state or abroad marry here all the time. The license is valid anywhere in California for 90 days.

How long does the ceremony take?

The ceremony itself is brief, a few minutes. Plan on being at the office for longer than that with check-in, paperwork, and any wait, especially at the downtown office.

How much does it cost to get married at the courthouse in San Diego?

$236 for most couples: $129 for a public marriage license and $107 for the Clerk's civil ceremony. A confidential license is $144 instead of $129. If you need the Clerk to provide your witness, add $58.

Do we need a witness?

With a public license, yes: one witness at the ceremony. Bring your own, the Clerk provides one for $58, or if I am photographing your ceremony, I sign as your witness at no charge. A confidential license needs no witness, but you must already live together.

Is there a justice of the peace in San Diego?

No. California does not have justices of the peace. A civil marriage in San Diego is performed by a deputy commissioner at the County Clerk's office, or by any authorized officiant you choose.

Can a friend marry us instead of the Clerk?

Yes. The Deputy Marriage Commissioner for a Day program lets the Clerk authorize a friend or relative to perform your ceremony at a venue of your choosing in California. Apply through the Clerk's office ahead of time.

Can we bring family?

Yes. Indoor rooms hold 15 to 20 guests depending on the office. Outdoor ceremonies downtown have no guest limit.

Can we have a champagne toast or throw petals?

Not at the Clerk's ceremony areas. Alcohol, tossing, and decor are not permitted. Do both a short walk away afterward, at the portrait spot.

How do we get the marriage certificate?

After the ceremony the license is registered, which can take up to 10 business days. Then order a certified copy from the Clerk. One is not sent automatically.

Is there parking downtown?

Yes. Free underground parking for up to 3 hours during business hours while on county business, with the garage entrance on Ash Street. Metered parking around Waterfront Park is the backup.

Does the Clerk perform ceremonies on weekends?

Once a month, on a Saturday, at the Santee office only, 9am to 3pm, by appointment. The 2026 dates are listed above. Every other ceremony is on a weekday.

Do you say vows at a civil ceremony?

Yes. The Clerk leads a short ceremony with vows you repeat, the ring exchange if you have rings, and the pronouncement. You can ask to add a few personal lines, and in my experience the officiants are kind about it, but keep them short. It is a real ceremony, not a signature.

Is there an actual ceremony or just paperwork?

An actual ceremony. California requires the marriage to be solemnized, so even the briefest courthouse wedding has an officiant, vows, and a pronouncement. The paperwork happens before and after it.

Is a courthouse wedding cheaper than a chapel or an officiant elopement?

Usually, yes. The Clerk's route is $236 in county fees for the license and ceremony. A private officiant who issues the license and performs the ceremony charges more for the convenience and the same-day timing, and a chapel adds the room on top. Photography is the same price at any of them.

What is the difference between the marriage license and the marriage certificate?

The license is the permission to marry, issued before the ceremony and valid for 90 days. Once it is signed and registered, the county issues the certificate, which is the proof you were married. You order certified copies of the certificate afterward; they are not mailed automatically.

How do we make a courthouse wedding feel special?

Dress like it matters, bring the two or three people who matter most, and plan the hour after. A walk to the waterfront for portraits, the toast and the petals at the portrait spot, then lunch in Little Italy turns a fifteen-minute appointment into a wedding day. Photos are how it stays that way.

Which office is best?

Downtown for the outdoor arbor, the marble lobby, and the bay a short walk away. Santee if you need a Saturday. San Marcos for North County and Camp Pendleton. Chula Vista for the South Bay. Same fees, same ceremony, and I photograph at all of them for the same price.

Where do we take photos near the San Marcos office?

The office itself is plain, so plan a second stop ten minutes away: the lakeside paths at Lake San Marcos, the oaks and hills of the Twin Oaks Valley, or Discovery Lake at golden hour. I will have a spot in mind for the time of day you booked.
