How the timeline is built

The builder starts from your ceremony time and works outward in both directions. Everything before the ceremony is counted backward, so you see when hair and makeup need to be finished. Everything after is counted forward to the end of the reception.

  • A first look moves the photos earlier. With a first look, the portraits, the wedding party, and the family photos happen before the ceremony, and you walk into cocktail hour with your guests. Without one, those photos happen during cocktail hour.
  • Family photos are counted per grouping. Each different mix of people is one grouping. A written list, and one person who knows both families calling names, keeps this part moving.
  • You are tucked away before the ceremony. The builder keeps the stretch before the ceremony free of posed photos, so guests do not see you as they arrive.
  • Golden hour gets its own slot. Give the builder a sunset time and it places a short portrait break in the warm light before sunset, or tells you when the ceremony itself lands in that light.
  • Lines marked "no photographer needed" are drives, and the part of the reception after your coverage ends.

How many hours of photography the day needs

The builder counts from the first photo to the last one and rounds up to the half hour. That number is the coverage to ask a photographer for. My wedding collections come in three lengths:

Eloping or marrying at the courthouse?

Those days are shorter, one to three hours, and they are planned around the light instead of a reception. The San Diego elopement spots guide has a golden hour finder for each spot, and the courthouse guide has a planner for the County Clerk appointment and fees.
